# G1 Pass 3 — Minimal Organization-Component Basis

**Role:** Main Research  
**Gate:** G1 — Mathematical Definition  
**Status:** Controller-authorized research artifact  
**Null programme:** \(H_0=\) known physics/chemistry. No \(\Phi\), G2 bound, or life definition is introduced.

## 1. Objective and decision rule

The task is to adversarially reduce candidate axes for the scale-indexed organization profile
\[
\mathfrak O_{\mathcal S}(\ell,\tau)=\{O_a(\mathcal S;\ell,\tau)\}_{a\in A}.
\]

A component is retained only if, on the admitted G1 model/counterexample class, removing it loses a mathematically relevant distinction not represented by the remaining axes. This is **basis minimality relative to the current admitted class**, not a theorem of universal logical necessity.

A component may be high in a nonliving system and still be a useful axis. Such a component is a **descriptor**, not a sufficient discriminator.

## 2. Candidate families and reduction

### A. Endogenous maintenance / renewal — RETAIN

**Object/domain.** Boundary-relative maintenance certificate data on the canonical stochastic reaction-compartment test class: loss challenge, internal restoration currents, intervention effects, provenance, and intervention-backed renewal dependency. EMC/RCC/CRDC remain provisional tests; the canonical axis records maintenance evidence rather than declaring EMC universal.

**Units.** Mixed: currents (events/time), probabilities/dimensionless intervention effects, graph structure.

**Scale.** Depends on horizon \(\tau\), boundary, maintained variables, and EMC-admissible observation map.

**Unique distinction.** Separates passive persistence and external repair/control from internally mediated renewal.

**High-value counterexample.** Internally self-repairing engineered machine can score high while nonliving.

**Redundancy test.** Catalytic closure, heredity, spatial compartmentalization, stability, information and causal dependence do not imply endogenous renewal. Removing this axis collapses passive/external persistence with internal renewal. **Retain.**

### B. Catalytic/reaction-network closure — RETAIN

**Object/domain.** On a catalytic reaction system \((X,R,C,F)\), RAF/subRAF structure is an established combinatorial object: reaction subsets that are reflexively autocatalytic and food-generated (S012–S013). On kinetic CRNs, reaction-current/stoichiometric refinements may supplement the combinatorial object.

**Units.** RAF membership/combinatorial structure dimensionless; kinetic currents have amount/time or events/time.

**Scale.** Depends on declared species/reaction resolution and food/environment partition; coarse-graining may create/destroy catalytic relations unless reaction provenance is retained.

**Unique distinction.** Detects collective catalytic production architecture that generic maintenance does not require: an engineered repair system may maintain itself without a chemically autocatalytic RAF.

**Counterexample.** Bare RAF/open autocatalytic chemistry can score high without compartment maintenance or heredity.

**Redundancy test.** Intervention-backed dependency closure is more general, but does not preserve the specifically chemical distinction between catalytic realization and arbitrary control/constraint dependency. Because the admitted test domain is chemical/reaction-compartmental, dropping catalysis loses reaction-mechanism structure. **Retain as a domain-specific profile axis.**

### C. Intervention-backed dependency/constraint closure — RETAIN

**Object/domain.** Directed graph \(G_D=(V,E)\) whose edges are admitted only when an intervention on mechanism/component \(j\) materially changes the declared support/maintenance statistic for \(i\), under explicit modularity assumptions. Closure summaries may include strongly connected components, cycle participation, or intervention-effect matrices.

**Units.** Graph structure dimensionless; edge weights may be probability differences, rates, or model-specific effect sizes.

**Scale.** Strongly representation-dependent. Requires provenance/intervention-preserving observation maps; arbitrary aggregation can manufacture cycles.

**Unique distinction.** Separates mere correlation or common driving from mechanistically supported dependency architecture, and generalizes beyond chemical catalysis.

**Counterexample.** Internally self-repairing engineered machines can exhibit strong dependency closure.

**Redundancy test.** Maintenance says that renewal occurs, but not the architecture of mutual dependence among constraints/components. Catalysis covers only one mechanism family. **Retain.**

### D. Memory — REJECT AS INDEPENDENT CANONICAL AXIS

**Object.** Conditional dependence on history, memory kernels, excess entropy, or state-retention statistics.

**Problem.** "Memory" is not one invariant mathematical object. Hidden-variable coarse-graining can generate effective memory; frozen disorder/static archives can store state without active organization; heredity and predictive information already capture two operationally sharper distinctions when applicable.

**Reduction.** Dynamic memory relevant to maintenance can be represented through path-state augmentation/dependency structure; transmissible persistent state belongs under heredity; generic temporal dependence belongs under predictive information.

**Decision.** **Do not retain generic memory as an independent basis axis.** Keep as a derived descriptor/subtype where a model specifies a memory object.

### E. Heredity / transmissible state — RETAIN, DOMAIN-CONDITIONAL

**Object/domain.** For systems with reproduction/transmission events, let parent state \(H_p\) and descendant state \(H_d\) be predeclared heritable variables. Candidate statistics include transmission mutual information \(I(H_p;H_d)\), fidelity/error kernels \(P(H_d|H_p)\), and persistence across generations. Eigen's quasispecies/error-threshold framework establishes a mathematically distinct heredity/fidelity regime (S026).

**Units.** Information in bits/nats; error/fidelity probabilities dimensionless.

**Scale.** Depends on choice of heritable variables, generation/event scale, and observation map. Data processing applies to deterministic/stochastic post-processing of the same parent/descendant variables, but changing what counts as a reproduction event is not mere coarse-graining.

**Unique distinction.** A replicator can transmit state without substantial self-maintenance; conversely an internally self-repairing machine can maintain itself without lineage transmission. Thus heredity is independent of maintenance.

**Counterexample.** Simple template replicator/high-fidelity copying system lacking autonomous maintenance.

**Redundancy test.** Predictive information within one trajectory does not imply cross-generation transmission; catalytic closure does not imply heredity. **Retain when a transmission/reproduction map exists; otherwise mark not-applicable, not zero.**

### F. Predictive information — REJECT FROM MINIMAL BASIS; RETAIN AS DESCRIPTOR

**Object.** \(I(X_{\rm past};X_{\rm future})\) or finite-window variants (S008).

**Units.** bits/nats.

**Scale.** Window/resolution dependent. For fixed variables, local post-processing obeys data-processing inequalities; temporal coarse-graining can otherwise change the comparison problem.

**Counterexamples.** Periodic oscillator, crystal/frozen system, externally controlled machine can have high predictability.

**Redundancy.** For the current basis, predictive information supplies a useful temporal-structure descriptor but no distinction needed by the adversarial model set that is not already represented by robustness/persistence, heredity where transmissible, and intervention-backed dependence. **Reject as independent minimal axis; retain as optional descriptor.**

### G. Causal/directed dependence — MERGE INTO DEPENDENCY/CLOSURE AXIS

Transfer entropy/conditional information (S007) quantify directed statistical dependence but can be confounded by hidden/common causes and do not equal interventionist causation. On the canonical test class, reaction-removal interventions provide a stronger provenance-backed dependency object.

**Decision.** Directed statistical measures remain diagnostics, not a separate basis axis. **Merge into C.**

### H. Spatial organization / compartmentalization — RETAIN

**Object/domain.** Boundary/compartment variables, spatial density fields, interface geometry, compartment membership and spatial correlation/segregation measures. On the canonical reaction-compartment class, a minimal axis records whether boundary variables mechanistically regulate retention/transport and whether maintained components are spatially localized relative to \(\mathcal B\).

**Units.** Geometry-dependent: length/area/volume, dimensionless partition/segregation statistics, or graph compartment labels.

**Scale.** Explicitly \(\ell\)-dependent. Spatial averaging can erase compartments/interfaces. No universal monotonicity.

**Unique distinction.** Bare/open autocatalytic chemistry and compartment-maintaining reaction networks can share catalytic closure but differ in maintained spatial localization/boundary organization.

**Counterexamples.** Equilibrium crystals, convection cells, passive vesicles can show strong spatial organization without maintenance/heredity.

**Redundancy test.** Maintenance does not imply spatial compartmentalization; catalysis does not imply a maintained boundary. **Retain.**

### I. Topology — REJECT AS GENERIC INDEPENDENT AXIS

Persistent homology/topological invariants are rigorous descriptors (S015), but physically meaningful topology is representation-specific. Current counterexamples do not reveal a distinction requiring a topology axis once spatial/compartment organization and dependency-network structure are retained. Crystals/material defects/data clouds can have nontrivial topology.

**Decision.** **Reject as generic basis axis.** Admit topology as a model-specific subdescriptor of spatial or network organization when a physical invariant/filtration is justified.

### J. Dynamical robustness/stability — RETAIN

**Object/domain.** Model-appropriate perturbation/return/survival statistics: e.g. viability return probability after a declared perturbation, mean first-exit/return time, Lyapunov/spectral quantities where defined.

**Units.** probabilities dimensionless; times have time units; exponents inverse time.

**Scale.** Depends on perturbation class, horizon and observation map. Coarse-graining can alter apparent stability; no universal monotonicity.

**Unique distinction.** Maintenance architecture and catalysis can exist yet be dynamically fragile. Robustness asks whether organization persists/returns under perturbation, not whether renewal mechanisms exist.

**Counterexamples.** Crystal and oscillator can be highly stable/robust without maintenance.

**Redundancy test.** EMC intervention effects do not characterize general perturbation response. **Retain.**

### K. Cross-scale coupling / multiscale dependence — RETAIN AS STRUCTURAL AXIS

**Object/domain.** Dependence between explicitly nested scale representations, for example \(I(Z_\ell;Z_L)\) for declared variables with \(\ell<L\), cross-scale response/conditional-dependence matrices, or persistence of mechanism/provenance across a commuting family of observation maps. No single universal statistic is canonical.

**Units.** measure-dependent; information variants bits/nats, response variants model-specific.

**Scale.** Intrinsically indexed by scale pairs. Data processing applies only for nested stochastic maps forming an actual Markov chain of representations; otherwise no monotonicity is assumed.

**Unique distinction.** Two systems can match local maintenance/catalysis/spatial statistics at one resolution while differing in whether local processes constrain or support mesoscopic/global organization. This is precisely the multiscale distinction the project must preserve before G2.

**Counterexamples.** Turbulence exhibits strong cross-scale coupling without endogenous maintenance; engineered hierarchical systems can as well.

**Redundancy test.** No single-scale component reconstructs cross-scale dependence in general. **Retain**, while leaving its exact statistic family provisional.

## 3. Reduced basis

The minimal candidate basis relative to the current admitted domain/counterexample set is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak O_{\mathcal S}(\ell,\tau)
=
\{
O_{\rm maint},
O_{\rm cat},
O_{\rm dep},
O_{\rm hered},
O_{\rm spatial},
O_{\rm robust},
O_{\rm xscale}
\}_{\ell,\tau}
}
\]

with \(O_{\rm hered}\) domain-conditional (N/A where no transmission event exists).

These are **axes**, not sufficient conditions and not a scalar. Exact numeric representatives may be families rather than single numbers.

Rejected/merged from the minimal basis:
- generic memory: derived/subtyped;
- predictive information: optional temporal descriptor;
- directed statistical dependence: diagnostic merged into dependency axis;
- generic topology: model-specific subdescriptor;
- generic correlation/information/dissipation: descriptors already rejected as organization definitions.

### Matrix result

No retained column is dominated by the others over this model family:

- **Maintenance** uniquely separates passive/external persistence from internal renewal.
- **Catalysis** separates chemically collective production from noncatalytic engineered maintenance.
- **Dependency closure** distinguishes mechanistic mutual-support architecture from mere catalytic membership or state correlation.
- **Heredity** distinguishes lineage/transmission structure from within-lifetime maintenance.
- **Spatial/compartment** distinguishes bare autocatalysis from maintained localization/boundary organization.
- **Robustness** distinguishes fragile from perturbation-resistant realizations with similar architecture.
- **Cross-scale** distinguishes single-scale organization from coupling among resolutions.

The matrix also proves no retained axis is sufficient: every column has high-valued nonliving counterexamples.

## 5. Removal tests

1. Remove maintenance -> external repair and endogenous renewal become indistinguishable. FAIL.
2. Remove catalysis -> chemical autocatalytic architecture collapses into generic dependency/maintenance. FAIL on admitted chemical domain.
3. Remove dependency closure -> correlation/catalysis cannot represent intervention-backed mutual support across heterogeneous mechanisms. FAIL.
4. Remove heredity -> replicator transmission becomes invisible. FAIL where reproduction/transmission is admitted.
5. Remove spatial/compartment -> bare autocatalytic and compartment-maintaining networks become indistinguishable. FAIL.
6. Remove robustness -> fragile and perturbation-resistant organizations with same architecture become indistinguishable. FAIL.
7. Remove cross-scale -> systems matching at one scale but differing in scale-to-scale dependence become indistinguishable. FAIL.

By contrast:
- remove generic memory -> transmissible memory remains under heredity; dynamic/path dependence remains available as descriptor/dependency. No required matrix distinction lost.
- remove predictive information -> oscillator predictability no longer creates a basis axis; no required organizational distinction lost.
- remove generic topology -> spatial/network subdescriptors retain physically grounded distinctions.
- remove transfer entropy as axis -> intervention-backed dependency remains.

## 6. Multiscale transformation audit

| Axis | Under \(C_{\ell,\tau}\) |
|---|---|
| maintenance | requires EMC/provenance-admissible maps for verdict transport; otherwise may become unidentifiable |
| catalysis | requires reaction/species provenance; aggregation may create/destroy apparent catalytic relations |
| dependency closure | requires intervention mapping/provenance; arbitrary aggregation can create/destroy cycles |
| heredity | data processing can bound \(I(H_p;H_d)\) under fixed post-processing maps; event/variable redefinition changes the object |
| spatial/compartment | explicitly scale-dependent; coarse spatial averaging may erase interfaces/localization; no universal monotonicity |
| robustness | perturbation and observed state class must be transported explicitly; no universal monotonicity |
| cross-scale | defined on scale pairs; information variants obey DPI only for genuine nested Markov observation chains |

Thus only selected information-theoretic representatives inherit standard data-processing monotonicity. Universal organization monotonicity remains rejected.

## 7. Canonicalization

### Earned canonical profile-component status

Canonical as **axes/types**, not scalar scores or life criteria:

1. endogenous maintenance/renewal;
2. catalytic organization;
3. intervention-backed dependency/constraint organization;
4. heredity/transmissible-state organization (domain-conditional);
5. spatial/compartment organization;
6. dynamical robustness;
7. cross-scale organization/dependence.

Each axis must be instantiated by a declared mathematical observable family on its domain. "N/A" is allowed where an axis requires a structure absent from the model (notably heredity).

### Not canonical as independent axes

Generic memory, predictive information, directed statistical dependence, topology, generic correlation, entropy/information content, and dissipation. These remain useful descriptors/diagnostics/subcomponents where justified.

## 9. Strongest findings

**Strongest redundancy finding:** generic memory and predictive information do not survive as independent basis axes. They are mathematically useful descriptors, but the current counterexample set does not require them once heredity, robustness and intervention-backed dependency are retained.

**Strongest counterexample:** the internally self-repairing engineered machine remains decisive. It can be high in maintenance, dependency closure and robustness, demonstrating that the profile is not a disguised definition of life and that additional axes remain independently meaningful.
